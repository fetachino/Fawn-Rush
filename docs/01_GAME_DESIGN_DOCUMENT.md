# 01 — Game Design Document

**Fawn Rush** — *Every crossing is a new adventure.*

Version 1.0 · Phase 1 baseline · Owner: Game Director

---

## 1. Identity

Fawn Rush is a game about being small in a world built for something bigger. You are a fawn — later a
fox, a hedgehog, a duck, a wolf pup — standing at the edge of a road with a horizon of headlights.
You take one step. Then one more. The world does not stop for you.

The emotional target is **nervous delight**: the specific thrill of a gap opening in traffic and the
half-second where you decide whether it is wide enough. It is cozy in its art and ruthless in its
timing. The tension is entirely generated by other things ignoring you.

**Positioning.** Roblox has many endless runners and many pet collectors. Fawn Rush is neither an
obstacle-dodger with a pet shop bolted on, nor a collection game with a minigame attached. It is a
crossing game — a genre with real legacy (lane-by-lane, committed moves, one-lane-at-a-time tension) —
that uses collection as its long arc. The collection is not a shop; it is a cast. Each animal crosses
differently *in feel* while being identical *in rules* (D5), so the game never becomes pay-to-win and
never punishes you for loving a Common.

**Three sentences a player would say about it:**

- "It's the frog game but with animals you actually want to collect."
- "The near-miss sound is so good, it makes me greedy."
- "I've almost got the wolf pup — one more good run in the snow."

---

## 2. Design pillars

Every feature must serve at least one pillar or it is cut.

**P1 — Fair or it doesn't ship.**
Death must always be a decision the player could have seen and avoided. This is a *technical*
promise (§7, and `04` §6) enforced by a path solver and a lag-compensation window, not a marketing
line. A player who dies to an unavoidable wall is a player we failed.

**P2 — One more crossing.**
The retry loop must be faster than the hesitation loop. Death → score → retry is under 2.5 seconds of
non-gameplay. The run is short enough (90 s typical) that stopping feels arbitrary.

**P3 — Skilled greed is rewarded, safe play is respected.**
Near-miss combos pay for risk; safe, patient crossing still pays steadily. Neither strategy is
strictly dominant. No mechanic punishes caution with a timer.

**P4 — A cast, not a shop.**
Animals are characters with personality — animation, sound, idle behaviour — not inventory rows.
A player should be able to name their favourite Common animal.

**P5 — Alive, not loud.**
The lobby is full of other players' animals doing idle animations. The world is full of ambient
motion. But nothing shakes the camera hard, nothing flashes white, and no effect ever hides the road.

**P6 — Respect the player.**
No friction timers sold back to you. No energy system. No countdown that resets a streak. Missions
progress for free. Purchases are disclosed in plain words.

## 3. Audience and platform

**Primary.** Roblox players 9–15 on iOS/Android tablets and phones, playing in 5–20 minute sessions,
usually with friends in the server or on a call. They already know Roblox conventions: a Play button,
a shop, daily rewards, a collection grid. They will leave instantly if the first run feels floaty or
if the UI is unreadable on a phone.

**Secondary.** 16–24 desktop players chasing leaderboards; controller and console players who expect
full input parity.

**Device priorities.** Mobile landscape → desktop → tablet → controller. Performance target: stable
30 FPS on a 2019-era mid-range Android at graphics level 4, 60 FPS on desktop at level 8. See `09` §5.2.

**Session shape.** 1–3 runs per visit early on; 3–8 once a player has a target animal. Total session
target 6–14 minutes. Daily return driven by the mission set and one "chase" animal.

## 4. Loops

### 4.1 The run (30 seconds — 3 minutes)

Step forward. Read the traffic. Commit. Every hazard you survive puts you one row closer to a better
reward tier; every near-miss adds to a combo that multiplies it. The run ends when a vehicle hits you
or you choose to bank. Banking is the only way to keep combo-earned acorns — **dying costs you the
combo, not your acorns** (see §12.4).

### 4.2 The session (8 — 15 minutes)

Enter → lobby shows your animal and everyone else's → daily missions are visible on the Play card →
pick an unlocked world → run → results → retry or return → progress bars for level, missions, and the
next animal are all one tap away. Two or three runs later, a mission completes and pays out with a
full-screen reward moment.

### 4.3 The week

Weekly challenges (bigger targets, notable rewards), one rotating world spotlight with a +25% acorn
modifier, weekly leaderboard reset, and a login calendar that advances on claim rather than expiring.

### 4.4 The arc (months)

Collect all animals → equip and customise → clear each world's row-depth achievement → chase Mythic
and Secret → seasonal cosmetic sets → leaderboard placement. The arc is horizontal: progression is
optional depth, never a gate on the core loop.

## 5. The run, moment to moment

**Camera.** Behind and above the animal, low enough that approaching traffic reads as *incoming*
rather than *overhead*. It leads the animal by a fraction of a row so the next corridor is already
visible. Details in `04` §3.

**Movement.** One input = one tile step. `TILE` = 4 studs. Step animation 0.11 s, input cooldown
0.10 s, so a held key and a fast swipe both produce a smooth cadence rather than a stutter. A single
queued input is buffered for 0.18 s so an early press is not lost — this is the difference between
"responsive" and "unresponsive" in playtest notes. Diagonal input resolves to the *most recent axis
pressed*, never a diagonal step.

**Threat reading.** Vehicles spawn off-screen with enough lead time that a player standing on a
median can see the next corridor resolve before committing. Lane direction is communicated by vehicle
orientation and a subtle road arrow decal. Trains are announced 1.2 s before arrival by horn, rails
trembling, and a light bar — trains never kill someone who was watching.

**Death.** Not an instant cut. 0.12 s of impact freeze on the exact contact frame, the animal's
species-specific failure animation, a desaturated flash, then the results card. The camera pulls back
a little so the player sees *what hit them*. This is the single most-resented moment in the genre when
handled badly, so it gets explicit attention (`05` §9.6).

**Collectibles.** Acorns spawn on safe rows and on road lanes. A road acorn is worth 3× a safe-row
acorn, and picking one usually means standing in traffic for one extra beat. That single exchange —
*one more step for triple pay* — is the game's risk dial and needs no tutorial.

## 6. The character

The default animal is a **baby deer**. The fawn is the game's mascot and its tutorial: everything the
player learns about animation quality, they learn from the fawn.

### 6.1 Fawn design

Proportions chosen for readability at 4 studs tall in a third-person camera at 20 studs distance:
oversized head (1.5 : 1 head-to-body), long thin legs, ears roughly a third of head height, a short
tail with a bright underside. Silhouette must be identifiable as a black shape against the road at
54 px tall on a phone screen.

**Expressive eyes.** Large, forward-facing, with an iris that maps a virtual 2D "look target" so the
eyes track the nearest hazard. Blinks are irregular (2.4–6.0 s, randomised) with occasional double
blinks. Eyes narrow slightly during a near-miss, widen at spawn, and close on death.

**Animated ears.** Two-bone ear chains, procedurally driven: a constant low-amplitude sway, plus
directional flicks in response to events (horn → both ears snap back; near-miss → one ear rotates to
track the vehicle; idle → a slow turn toward whatever the head is looking at).

**Moving tail.** A three-bone tail with an overlapping sine chain and a fast shake state used for the
happy and victory animations. Tail motion is always slightly behind the body — the primary "alive"
tell.

### 6.2 Animation set (required for every animal)

Each animation is a state in a small animation controller with cross-fades, never hard cuts.

| # | State | Duration / loop | Trigger | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Idle breathing | 3.2 s loop | Default on safe rows | Chest scale 0.98→1.02, head bob 0.5° |
| 2 | Idle alert | 1.6 s loop | Standing in a vehicle lane | Ears up, body 4% lower, eyes track hazard |
| 3 | Run | 0.11 s per step | Step accepted | Leg IK cycle phase-locked to step cadence |
| 4 | Jump | 0.34 s | `Space` / jump button | Tuck, then stretch; apex at 45% |
| 5 | Land | 0.14 s | Jump landing | Absorption squash of 8% |
| 6 | Frightened | 0.4 s | Hazard passes within 1.5 tiles | Freeze, ears flat, eyes wide; **does not interrupt movement** |
| 7 | Stumble | 0.3 s | Grazing a hazard in a forgiving position | Off-balance lean, one leg catch |
| 8 | Happy | 1.2 s | Mission complete / unlock / daily claim | Head lift, tail shake, 2 hops |
| 9 | Death / failure | 1.1 s | Collision confirmed | Species-specific; cartoon impact, no gore |
| 10 | Victory | 1.6 s | Personal best / world clear | Rear-up, front paws out, head back |

States 1–7 are shared behaviour with per-species body-language overrides (a hedgehog's "run" is a
scurry, a duck's "jump" is a flap-assisted hop). States 8–10 are authored per species.

**Rule:** the frightened animation never interrupts the step tween. Reacting to a threat must never
cost a frame of control — that is precisely the moment the player needs control most.

### 6.3 Feel requirements (acceptance criteria)

A reviewer signs off on the fawn only when all of these are true, checked on a phone:

- The fawn's silhouette is distinguishable from a fox at 100 px on screen.
- Standing still for 10 s never shows a repeated identical blink pattern.
- Eye tracking points at the closest hazard, not the closest *moving* thing.
- The step cadence is identical whether the player taps, swipes, or holds a key.
- The death animation reads as *comic surprise*, not as punishment.

## 7. Hazards and the fairness doctrine

### 7.1 Hazard roster

| # | Hazard | Behaviour | Introduced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compact car | Baseline speed, 2 tiles long | World 1, row 0 |
| 2 | Fast sports car | 1.7× baseline speed, 2 tiles, late-spawned | World 1, row 40 |
| 3 | Pickup truck | 3 tiles long, slow, wide gap behind | World 1, row 80 |
| 4 | Bus | 4 tiles long, slow, stops at a shelter | World 2, row 0 |
| 5 | Motorcycle | Very fast, 1 tile, tight spacing pairs | World 2, row 60 |
| 6 | Delivery van | Medium, erratic lane changes (telegraphed) | World 2, row 120 |
| 7 | Train | 10 tiles, 3-lane-wide streak, announced 1.2 s ahead | World 2, row 180 |
| 8 | Moving obstacle | Wildlife crossing the lane perpendicular (deer, tumbleweed, crabs) | World 1, row 120 |
| 9 | Road closure | Static barrier + cone field forcing a lane shift; always with a visible detour | World 3, row 0 |
| 10 | Event hazard | Rotating seasonal manipulators (dune storm, blizzard gust, neon gate) | World 5 / events |

Hazards 8 and 9 are **static or slow** and exist specifically so every corridor has a readable,
non-lethal element. Hazard 10 is always purely cosmetic-plus-timing: a gust changes vehicle *speed*
visibly for 3 s with a wind trail, never position.

### 7.2 The fairness doctrine

Five invariants. They are enforced by generation-time validation (`04` §6), not by luck.

1. **Every corridor is survivable.** A corridor is 1–3 lanes; a median always follows. The
   `SafePathValidator` runs a time-indexed path search and rejects any chunk without a solution.
2. **No unsignalled hazard.** Trains, lane-closing barriers, and event gusts are announced by a
   visible and audible cue at least 1.0 s before they can kill.
3. **No player-caused walls.** Vehicles never brake or steer *because of the player*; traffic is
   independent and therefore predictably independent.
4. **No death without a decision.** If a step is accepted by the server, and the step's target tile is
   occupied at the moment of acceptance by traffic the player could not have seen when they pressed
   the key, the server rewinds by up to `REWIND_MAX_MS` and favours the player.
5. **The cap holds.** Difficulty clamps at each world's `RAMP_ROWS`. Late-game is "hard forever", never
   "impossible at minute nine".

### 7.3 Difficulty curve

Difficulty is a single scalar `D(row)` in [0, 1]:

```
D(row) = clamp((row / RAMP_ROWS) ^ 0.85, 0, 1)
```

`D` drives four things simultaneously, with separate curves so the late game is not "everything at
once":

| Driver | Formula (defaults, see `EconomyConfig` / `WorldConfig`) | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle speed | `lerp(vMin, vMax, D^0.9) × worldSpeedMul × (1 ± 0.08 jitter)` | 9 → 26 studs/s |
| Traffic density | `lerp(gapMulMin=1.00, gapMulMax=0.62, D)` shrinks headway, floored by the fairness gap | 1.00 → 0.62 |
| Corridor width | Weighted random, weights shift from `{1:80,2:20,3:0}` to `{1:15,2:45,3:40}` | 1–3 lanes |
| Hazard variety | New types unlock at fixed rows, never at random | see §7.1 |

`RAMP_ROWS` per world: Greenwood 500, City 560, Desert 600, Snowy Summit 640, Neon Night 700.

### 7.4 Near-miss system

A near-miss is registered when a hazard's edge passes within `NEAR_MISS_TILES` (default 0.75) of the
player's collision box, while the player is in that hazard's lane, on a live run. Detection is
server-side on the same tick that resolves collisions, so a near-miss and a death are mutually
exclusive — you cannot be rewarded for dying.

| Effect | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Sound | Bright "whoosh" with a 3-semitone pitch rise per combo tier |
| Visual | 90 ms white rim vignette at 12% opacity; 4–6 speed-line particles along the hazard's axis |
| Shake | Camera **offset** of 0.12 studs for 60 ms — never rotational shake |
| Currency | `NEAR_MISS_ACORNS (2) × combo multiplier`, capped at 12 per event |
| Combo | `+1` step on a 0–4 ladder: ×1.0, ×1.25, ×1.6, ×2.1, ×3.0; ×5.0 at 5+ consecutive |
| Decay | Combo resets after `COMBO_DECAY` (6.0 s) with no event; shown as a draining ring |
| Stats | `nearMisses`, `bestCombo`, `bestSingleRunCombo` persisted per player |

Combo affects **acorns and score only**, never XP rate or mission weighting beyond its own mission.
Combo is banked on a voluntary return to the lobby, lost on death — this is the game's one loss
condition and it is stated plainly in the results screen and the tutorial.

## 8. Worlds

Five at launch. Each is a config profile plus a scenery builder (`WorldConfig` + `WorldBuilder/*`), so
a sixth world is data plus one builder module — no core gameplay change (requirement: `02` §8,
proven at the `08` Phase 4 exit criteria).

| | World | Theme | Palette | Traffic profile | Music | Unlock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Greenwood Crossing** | Sunlit forest, wooden signs, ponds | Moss, cream, sunlit gold | Gentle, wide medians, slowest ramp | Acoustic guitar + soft pads | Default |
| 2 | **City Chaos** | Downtown, taxis, traffic lights, crosswalks | Concrete, taxi yellow, signage red | Faster, narrow medians, buses + trains | Upbeat urban percussion | Player level 5 |
| 3 | **Desert Dash** | Canyon highway, cacti, heat haze | Sand, terracotta, bleached bone | Sparse traffic, high speed, wide lanes | Adventure strings + frame drum | Level 12 **and** 3,000 lifetime rows |
| 4 | **Snowy Summit** | Frozen mountain pass, snowfall, ice | Blue-white, pine, slate | Slow vehicles with long slides, low grip telegraphs | Peaceful winter ambience | Level 20 |
| 5 | **Neon Night** | Rain-slick neon boulevard, hover traffic | Magenta, cyan, deep violet | Everything fast, 3-lane corridors common | Synthwave, side-chained | Level 30 **and** 8,000 lifetime rows |

Each world supplies, via its profile:

- **Scenery builder** — deterministic, seeded, pooled props (trees, buildings, cacti, pines, holo-ads).
- **Ambient sound bed** — one looping ambience plus 3–6 random one-shots on a jittered timer.
- **Music track** — lobby track shared; per-world track cross-faded on run start (2.0 s fade).
- **Collectible skin** — acorn, coin, cactus nut, ice gem, neon shard. Values are identical across
  worlds; only the model and pickup sound differ. **No world pays better per row** — that would make
  the first world pointless.
- **Difficulty profile** — `vMin`, `vMax`, `RAMP_ROWS`, corridor weights, hazard table.
- **World achievement set** — 3 per world (rows reached, no-death streak, collectible sweep).

**World transitions.** A run stays inside one world. Between worlds, the lobby uses a themed backdrop
and a short camera flythrough on unlock. Chaining worlds inside a single run (**Expedition**) is
specified as a future mode in §11.4 and is not in the Phase 1 scope.

### 9.2 Rarity presentation

Rarity is communicated on four channels at once; a colour label alone is a failure.

| Rarity | Colour | Card treatment | Unlock reveal | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common | `#B8C2CC` slate | Flat card, 8 px radius | Simple pop, 0.6 s | Single soft chime |
| Uncommon | `#5FBF6A` leaf | Flat card, 8 px, leaf corner | Pop + leaf burst, 0.9 s | Two-note chime |
| Rare | `#4A90E2` sky | Gradient edge, 14 px | Spinning reveal, 1.4 s | Three-note arpeggio |
| Epic | `#A45BE0` violet | Gradient edge + corner filigree | Radial light burst, 2.0 s | Arpeggio + shimmer tail |
| Legendary | `#F2A33C` gold | Animated gold sweep on card | Slow-motion reveal, 2.6 s, screen dims to 40% | Brass motif |
| Mythic | `#FF4D6D` crimson | Prismatic border, pulse | Full-screen radial bloom + particles, 3.2 s | Choir swell |
| Secret | `#3ECFB2` teal prism | Prismatic + unique diagonal cut | Held reveal, 3.6 s, silent for 0.4 s then shimmer | Reverse reverb sting |

The colour is *never* the only signal: each rarity also has a distinct border shape (flat, notched,
bevel, double-bevel, filigree, prismatic, diagonal-cut) and a distinct card animation speed. This is
the colour-blind requirement (`05` §11).

### 9.3 Collection UI requirements

Search by name (string contains, case-insensitive, debounced 120 ms). Filters: rarity (multi-select),
owned/unowned/all, unlocked/locked-by-condition. Sort: rarity, name, acquisition date, recently
unlocked. Tile shows a `ViewportFrame` with the live rigged model, gently rotating at 18°/s, pausable
by touch and draggable for a full 360°. Tap → detail sheet with description, unlock method, owned
state, equip button, and (if owned and equipped) an "unlink" state.

Preview performance: **at most 4 `ViewportFrame`s alive at once**; the list virtualises beyond that,
showing a static cached thumbnail. This is a hard mobile requirement (`09` §5.4).

## 10. Customisation

| Slot | Examples | Compatibility rule |
|---|---|---|
| Hat | Acorn cap, leaf beret, aviator goggles, crown | Anchored to `Head` attach, scaled by head-sphere radius |
| Face | Glasses, monocle, eyepatch, blush | Uses eye anchors + face offset table |
| Neck | Scarf, bandana, bell collar, bow tie | Anchored to `Neck` with species neck thickness |
| Back | Backpack, sprout, tiny cape, kite | `Spine` attach with species offset |
| Feet | Sneakers, boots, socks, tiny skates | Four foot anchors, mirrored |
| Trail | Leaf trail, sparkle, paw prints, neon ribbon | Spawned from root, capped at 24 live particles |
| Aura | Fireflies, snow dust, embers | Single `ParticleEmitter` parented to root, 12 particles |
| Victory animation | Rear-up, spin, bow, backflip | Overrides animation state 10 |
| Emote | Wave, sit, dance, sleep | Plays in lobby only, 3 s, cancellable |
| Colour variant | 6 palettes per animal | Recolours the `Body` palette region only |

**Clipping control.** Every animal publishes an `attach` table (head radius, neck thickness, back
offset, foot offsets). Cosmetics are authored against a canonical proportion set and scaled per
species with a clamp (0.75–1.3). The pipeline explicitly does not attempt per-vertex deformation;
instead each cosmetic declares `fitsRig` tags and the UI greys out incompatible items with a reason
string ("doesn't fit long-eared animals").

Customisation is **free after unlock**. There is no dyeing cost, no per-equip fee, no cosmetic
durability, no attach-slot microtransaction. You paid for the hat; the hat is yours.

**Loadouts.** One default loadout free; three additional slots (a gamepass). Slots store the full
equipped set and the animal, so switching is one tap.

## 11. Modes

**11.1 Classified (default).** The run as described. Distance and score feed global and weekly boards.

**11.2 Practice.** Unlocked at level 3. No traffic-speed ramp, no rewards, no leaderboard. Exists so
an anxious new player can learn to read a corridor. Intentionally not a punished mode.

**11.3 Daily Gauntlet (Phase 8+).** One shared seed per day, identical traffic for everyone on that
server, score-only, one attempt. Seeds are generated server-side and stored, so the challenge is
genuinely identical. Rewards acorns + one Golden Acorn for the top 10 in-server.

**11.4 Expedition (deferred).** Chain worlds every 150 rows within one run, difficulty carried across
the seam. Explicitly out of Phase 1–10 scope; designed only to confirm no core change is required
later (world selection is already an `activeWorldProfile` parameter, and the scenery builder is
already swappable mid-run in the zone).

## 12. Economy

### 12.1 Two currencies

| | Acorns | Golden Acorns |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Soft currency: animals, cosmetics, caches | Hard currency: prestige cosmetics, exclusive trails |
| Earned by | Every run, missions, achievements, daily login, events | Long single runs (1 per 250 rows, max 2/run), weekly challenges (2 each), achievement tiers (5 for a category completion), world clears |
| Robux purchasable | **Yes** (developer products, small packs) | **No, never** (D6) |
| Cap | 9,999,999 (display clamps to `99.9K` etc.) | 9,999 |

### 12.2 Earnings per run (default tuning)

| Source | Value |
|---|---|
| Safe-row acorn | 1 |
| Road-lane acorn | 3 |
| Row bonus | +1 acorn per 2 rows, awarded on bank or death (never lost) |
| Near-miss | `2 × combo multiplier` (cap 12) |
| Run completion (any end) | 10 base |
| Mission | 60–250 acorns |
| Daily login | 25 → 400 across a 7-day cycle |

A median run (90 rows, ~14 pickups, ~8 near-misses) pays roughly **95–120 acorns**. With a
`2× Acorns` pass, that becomes 190–240.

### 12.3 Sinks

Animals 250–11,000 · cosmetics 400–6,000 · caches 750 (published odds, duplicate-protected) ·
trail recolours 200 · loadout restyling free. Sinks are deliberately front-loaded (the first animal
costs 250 and is affordable in two runs) so the loop proves itself before the grind.

### 12.4 Death and combo

Dying loses **only the combo multiplier**, never banked acorns, never XP, never mission progress.
Run-earned acorns are credited at run end whichever way the run ends. This is the single most
important economy decision in the game: it keeps runs cheap to attempt and keeps the sting on
*skill expression* instead of on *earned money*.

## 13. Progression

**XP per action:** +1 per row crossed, +5 per near-miss, +25 per run completed, mission rewards pay XP
in addition to acorns.

**Level curve:** `xpToNext(level) = round(150 × 1.13^(level−1))`, capped at 2,000 for level 40+.
Cumulative XP to level 30 ≈ 38,800, which at a median 200 XP/run is roughly 190 runs — about 5–6
hours of play. Sample values: L1 150, L5 244, L10 450, L20 1,529, L30 3,970 (capped).

**Level rewards:** every level pays acorns `40 + 12 × level`, plus a Golden Acorn at every 10th level,
a cosmetic at levels 5/15/25, and a milestone cache at 10/20/30/40. Levels gate worlds (§8) and the
Practice mode.

**Missions.** 3 daily (from a pool of 16 templates, one free reroll per day), 5 weekly (pool of 12).
Progress is server-tracked counters incremented from validated gameplay events only. Templates:
cross N rows, collect N acorns, complete N runs, survive N seconds in one run, land N near-misses in
one run, reach combo ×N, bank a run, play in a specific world, equip a hat, play with a friend in the
server, complete a run without dying before row 50.

**Daily login.** Seven-day calendar that **advances only on claim**. A missed day costs nothing — it
simply does not advance. Day 7 is a cosmetic; the cycle then loops with escalating acorns. No
countdown timers pressure the player, and the calendar never resets a streak.

**Achievements.** Six categories (Survival, Collection, Exploration, Skill, Social, Progression), ~34
entries, each with name, description, requirement, progress counter, reward, and completion state.
Every one is achievable without spending Robux. Full tables live in `AchievementConfig`.

**Roblox badges.** `[STUDIO]` Eight platform badges mapped to major achievements via
`BadgeService:AwardBadge`. IDs are empty until created; the award path no-ops and logs when unset.

## 14. Social and multiplayer

- **The lobby is the social space.** Every player in the server stands in a shared lobby with their
  equipped animal, cosmetics, nameplate, level, and title. You can emote, sit, and inspect other
  players' loadouts directly.
- **Runs are private.** Each player's run happens in their own zone (§4 of `02`), so nobody can block,
  push, or interfere. Watching is opt-in: the lobby has a "spectate" board that streams a low-frequency
  progress line for players who allow it. Out of scope for Phase 8 MVP; the data hook exists.
- **Leaderboards.** Global (all-time best row depth), weekly (resets Monday 00:00 UTC, `OrderedDataStore`
  per ISO week), friends (Roblox `GetFriendsOnline` / friend list filtering of the global board), and
  personal records (best run, best combo, total rows, total animals).
- **Titles.** Earned, displayed under the nameplate: "Untouched", "Snowbound", "Ten Thousand".
- **Friend joining.** Standard Roblox join flow; lobby shows friends in the server. No custom invite
  UI beyond a "Jump to friend" row in the leaderboard screen.
- **Anti-interference rule.** No client may modify another player's zone, currency, or run state. The
  server rejects any remote whose subject is not the sender (`02` §6).

## 15. Monetization philosophy

Full detail in `07`. The one-paragraph version:

Sell **convenience, cosmetics, and expression** — never survival. Five passes (VIP, Extra Loadout
Slots, Cosmetic Bundle, Premium Trails, Double Acorns) and five developer products (three acorn packs,
one revive, one limited cosmetic bundle). Nothing purchasable touches speed, hitbox, hazard density,
reward-per-row beyond the disclosed Double Acorns multiplier, or leaderboard eligibility. Golden
Acorns are never sold. Revives are single-use, run-scoped, capped at one per run, and explicitly do
not restore the lost combo.

## 16. Art direction

**Shape language.** Rounded, heavyweight, hand-placed. Animals are chunky and toy-like with three
silhouette tiers: round (rabbit, hedgehog, bear), wedge (fox, cat, wolf), long (deer, otter, duck).
Nothing is photoreal; nothing is a flat cube.

**Palette.** Warm foreground creatures against cooler environments, so the player's animal always
reads. Each world has a three-colour identity plus one accent (see §8). Global brand colours: Fawn
Gold `#F2B33D`, Moss `#4E7A3A`, Bark `#2B2119`, Cream `#FFF6E4`.

**Lighting.** `Future`-class lighting, soft shadows, per-world `Atmosphere`/`ColorCorrection`/
`Bloom` values that are *tuned down* on mobile graphics levels (a `GraphicsTier` config scales bloom
and particle rates). Never rely on bloom to make something readable.

**Readability first.** Hazard wheels must be dark enough to read against all five road surfaces;
collectibles must be brighter than their background by at least 30% luminance. Every art decision is
checked in a grayscale screenshot.

**No external IP.** All animals are genericised species, not characters from any franchise. Vehicle
designs are generic archetypes (a bus, not a specific city's bus). Logos on signage are invented
marks from the game's own fiction.

---

## 17. Audio direction

Music: a warm lobby theme (acoustic ensemble), and one track per world (§8). Every track is
licensed or original and cross-fades over 2.0 s. Gameplay SFX: footsteps (species-weighted), jump,
land, pickup (per-world timbre), vehicle pass-by (Doppler-ish, distance-attenuated), near-miss,
impact, level-up, unlock (per rarity), buttons, menu open/close, purchase success, achievement,
mission complete. Full inventory and placeholder policy in `06` §7.

**Mixing priorities** (server-side of the audio stack, but content must respect it): 1) death/impact,
2) near-miss, 3) pickups, 4) traffic approach, 5) music, 6) ambience, 7) UI. Three buses: `Music`,
`SFX`, `UI`, each with an independent 0–10 volume setting and a master mute; all persisted server-side.

## 18. Retention, KPIs and what we will not do

**Targets to measure from day one** (definitions and event names in `07`/`02` §10):

| Metric | Target | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| D1 retention | ≥ 28% | Genre baseline for Roblox casual with a daily mission set |
| D7 retention | ≥ 12% | |
| Median session | ≥ 7 min | |
| Runs per session | ≥ 3.2 | The retry loop is our core mechanic |
| First-run completion (rows ≥ 20) | ≥ 65% | Tutorial-adjacent; a fail here means an onboarding bug |
| Death-classified-unfair | ≤ 0.5% of deaths | Judged by player report tags + telemetry |
| ARPDAU | measured, not targeted | We do not tune the game around extracting it |

**What we explicitly will not do:**

- No energy/stamina or "you can play 3 more runs".
- No purchase-gated death insurance beyond the disclosed single revive.
- No randomised paid rewards.
- No fake scarcity ("only 4 left"), no fabricated discount timers.
- No punishing a returned player with a broken streak.
- No exploit of social pressure — no "your friend is ahead of you, buy X" nags.
- No dark-pattern notification spam via no-op remotes.

**Live operations.** Weekend acorn modifiers, seasonal worlds as *cosmetic skins* over existing world
profiles (so no new balance risk), rotating limited cosmetics that return annually, and a rotating
world spotlight (+25% acorns). Limited items are never required for progression.

---

## 19. Non-goals (v1)

Combat. Pets that affect gameplay. Trading. Chat-based economy. Guilds. Voice. Procedural mesh
generation at runtime. Any feature whose primary purpose is to increase session length against the
player's interest.
